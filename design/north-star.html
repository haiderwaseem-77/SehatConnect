1. What this is (mission)

  • Sehat Connect is a home-nursing & caregiving service in Lahore, Pakistan: verified nurses and trained attendants sent to families’ homes.
  • The website has one job: get a worried family member to leave their name + phone (or call / WhatsApp us) so a real person can call them back and arrange the care. It is a lead-generation + trust-building site — not a self-service booking app and not a marketplace.
  • Success = more qualified callbacks requested, and families trusting us enough to let our caregiver into their home. Every decision serves that.

2. Who we are designing for (always picture this person)

  • 40–65 years old, in Lahore. Usually a son/daughter or the person running the household, arranging care for an elderly parent — or for post-op recovery, a new mother & baby, a bedridden/long-term, dementia, or palliative patient.
  • Not very tech-savvy. Mobile-first, Android, often an older phone, ageing eyesight (cannot read small text). WhatsApp-native. Comfortable making a phone call. Uncomfortable with long forms, dropdowns, passwords/accounts, anything that feels like “signing up.”
  • Emotionally loaded: stressed, anxious, often in a hurry. A loved one is unwell.

The fears the site must defuse:

  1. “Is this real, or a scam?” (trust is the #1 conversion barrier in Pakistan)
  2. “Will a safe, decent person come into my home?” (especially for a female/elderly patient — they want a verified caregiver and often a female one)
  3. “Can this person actually do the medical task?” (a real qualified nurse)
  4. “How much will it cost — will I be overcharged?” (price transparency)
  5. “Can I just talk to a human?” (they don’t want to commit online; the phone call is the trust bridge)

3. The core principle (the one idea behind everything)

The customer is buying a trustworthy human, not software.
  • Spend money/effort on tech that makes our humans reliable and verifiable. Stay cheap on tech that tries to replace the human contact the customer actually wants.
  • Customer-facing = WhatsApp + a phone call + a dead-simple website. Anything fancy lives behind the scenes in internal staff tools.
  • The test for any feature: “Does this make a nervous family trust us more — or does it just look impressive?”

4. Design & experience rules (how it must look, work, and feel)

  • Big & readable: 18px base body text, nothing important under 16px, high-contrast dark text (not faint grey). Large tap targets (buttons ≥ 56px tall).
  • One page, one action: the home page does ~95% of the work; everything funnels to “we’ll call you.” No browsing, no accounts, no self-service booking calendar.
  • Call + WhatsApp are co-stars: reachable from anywhere (header call button + sticky bottom bar on mobile). Many users will never fill the form — they’ll WhatsApp. Pre-fill a friendly Roman-Urdu message for them.
  • The form asks for almost nothing: Name + Phone required. “Who needs care?” is optional, tap-not-type chips (never a long dropdown). Area is collected on the call. Every field removed lifts completion.
  • Bilingual: English default + an اردو toggle. Render the Urdu we already store in lib/constants.ts. English signals “professional/legit”; Urdu signals “I’m comfortable.” Use a proper Urdu font (Noto Nastaliq Urdu).
  • Prove trust, don’t claim it: show the price openly + “pay cash after, no advance,” a founder’s face & note, real testimonials (first name + area), “female nurse for female patient,” and simple “CNIC & references checked.” Real photos beat abstract icons.
  • Warm, calm, clean medical feel. Teal palette. Plain, short, human sentences.
  • Mobile-first and fast: assume a mid/low-end Android on patchy data.

5. What we build vs. what we skip (settled product decisions)

Idea Verdict Why
Pre-visit caregiver profile on WhatsApp (name + photo + verified badges + experience + ETA) BUILD — top priority Directly answers “who is this stranger coming to my home?” Highest trust-per-rupee; it’s just a message template.
Status updates (confirmed → assigned → on the way/ETA → arrived → done → “how was it?”) BUILD, but light WhatsApp-first (rich, two-way, carries the photo). SMS only as a fallback for the booking confirmation + ETA. Don’t build an SMS pipeline.
Same-caregiver continuity (“want the same nurse again? just tell us”) BUILD — no customer tech Tracked in our internal tool, requested on WhatsApp. Big for retention.
Female-for-female matching, clearly offered and honoured BUILD — no customer tech A real differentiator in this market.
Internal ops tool (lead inbox so no callback is dropped, caregiver roster/availability, verification records, shift logs, message templates) BUILD — the real backbone This is where dev resources actually pay off. The “dashboard” worth building is for staff, not customers.
Real-time GPS tracking SKIP High cost, marginal value for a 12-hour shift, caregiver privacy/labour friction, and it breaks visibly. The trust it seems to give comes cheaper from a verified profile + an ETA + a callable human.
Customer dashboard / login SKIP (public) This audience won’t log in to self-serve. WhatsApp + a human is faster and familiar — the WhatsApp thread IS their dashboard.
Browse-nurses / public nurse profiles SKIP Marketplace model: implies an inventory you must keep “available” and causes choice paralysis. “We match you” is more trustworthy and simpler to run.
Public ratings/reviews platform SKIP A few real testimonials on-site + a private post-shift “how did it go?” for quality control is enough.
Online payment (Easypaisa/JazzCash) OPTIONAL / LATER Cash-after is itself a trust feature (“no advance” = not a scam). Offer an e-wallet as a convenience for paying after; never require it upfront.

6. The honesty rule (no false promises)

  • Never promise on the site what we don’t actually deliver. Specifically: no GPS-tracking claims, no live “Available” roster, no customer-dashboard promises, no response times we can’t always hit. Reframe as the real thing: “updates by WhatsApp/SMS” + “a real person, one call away.”
  • The callback-time promise (e.g. “we’ll call within 15 minutes”) must be a number we hit every time. Pick a safe one — a missed promise here is the fastest way to lose this audience’s trust.

7. Language

English default, اردو toggle everywhere. Every user-facing string has an Urdu counterpart, rendered in the Urdu font (Noto Nastaliq Urdu).

8. The tests — every customer-facing change must pass these (Definition of Done)

A change is only “done” if all of these are true:

  1. The primary action is still “we’ll call you” (or call/WhatsApp). It does not introduce browsing, accounts, or self-service booking.
  2. The form stays minimal: Name + Phone required; anything else optional and tap-not-type.
  3. Call + WhatsApp are reachable from anywhere on the page (header + sticky bar). Phone/WhatsApp numbers come from constants — never hardcoded.
  4. Text is ≥ 16px (body 18px) and high-contrast; tap targets ≥ 48–56px. A 60-year-old can read it at arm’s length.
  5. It works well on a mid-range Android in portrait and is fast on slow data.
  6. Bilingual-ready: any new user-facing string has an Urdu counterpart and renders in the Urdu font.
  7. No false promises: nothing implies GPS, a live roster, a dashboard, or response times we don’t guarantee.
  8. Trust is shown with specifics (price, pay-after, real people), not just icon claims.
  9. Plain, warm language — short sentences a stressed, non-native-English reader understands instantly.
  10. It reuses existing constants/components and does not reintroduce the marketplace/account model.
