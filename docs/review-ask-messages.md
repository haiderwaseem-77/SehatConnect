# Asking for Google reviews

Reviews are the single biggest lever on map-pack ranking, and the one thing a
competitor cannot copy or buy. They also do something narrower and more useful:
a review that says *"nurse for my father in Johar Town"* tells Google this
business is relevant **for that service in that area**. Thirty reviews that all
say "good service" are worth far less than thirty that name what was done and
where.

Everything below assumes the Google Business Profile is verified and the review
short link exists. Get it from **Profile → Ask for reviews → copy link**, then
replace `<REVIEW_LINK>` in the messages.

---

## The rules that matter

**1. Never offer anything in exchange.** No discount, no free hour, no gift, not
even "we'll prioritise your next booking". Incentivised reviews violate Google's
policies and can get the reviews stripped and the profile penalised. It also
destroys the signal you are paying for.

**2. Ask everyone, not just the happy ones.** Selectively asking only satisfied
families ("review gating") is against Google's policies. Ask on a consistent
rule — for example, every family after their third shift — and let the results
be what they are. A profile with a handful of 3- and 4-star reviews among the
5s reads as real; a wall of flawless 5s reads as bought, to Google and to
families.

**3. Three to five a week, never a burst.** A brand-new profile that suddenly
receives twenty reviews gets them filtered or removed. Spread them out and keep
the pace steady — steady beats fast, permanently.

**4. Ask a person, not a household.** Send it to whoever actually booked and
spoke to you. "The family" never leaves a review; a named person sometimes does.

**5. Ask at the right moment.** The best time is right after someone thanks you
— end of a good shift, or when they extend a booking. Not on discharge day, not
mid-crisis.

**6. Send it from the number they already know.** The same WhatsApp they have
been talking to. A review request from an unknown number gets ignored.

**7. Reply to every review within 24 hours**, including the bad ones. Replies
are visible to families comparing you, and Google treats an active profile as a
maintained one.

## Message 1 — English

> Assalam-o-Alaikum [NAME],
>
> I hope [PATIENT] is doing better. It has been good to work with your family.
>
> If you were happy with the care, would you mind leaving us a short Google
> review? It genuinely helps other families in Lahore who are nervous about
> letting someone into their home — the way you were when you first called.
>
> If you can, please mention **which service you took and your area** — for
> example, *"attendant for my father in Johar Town"*. That detail is what helps
> other families nearby find us.
>
> Here is the link: <REVIEW_LINK>
>
> Thank you either way.
> — [YOUR NAME], Sehat Connect

>

>

## Follow-up — once only, after 3–4 days

Send this **once**. If there is no reply after it, stop. Chasing a review costs
more goodwill than the review is worth.

> Assalam-o-Alaikum [NAME] — just this one reminder, then I won't trouble you
> again. If you have a minute for a short Google review it would really help
> other families: <REVIEW_LINK>
>
> And if something about the care wasn't right, please tell me directly instead
> — I would rather fix it. — [YOUR NAME]

That second sentence is doing real work. It gives an unhappy family a private
route to complain, which is legitimate service recovery — and it is **not**
review gating, because the review link is still there for them to use either way.

---

## When a review is bad

Reply within 24 hours, publicly, without arguing:

> Thank you for telling us, [NAME] — I'm sorry this fell short. That is not the
> standard we hold ourselves to. I'd like to put it right; please call me
> directly on 0328-8489988. — [YOUR NAME], Sehat Connect

Then actually fix it. One handled complaint in public is worth more than several
generic five-star reviews, because it is the thing a nervous family is scanning
for.

**Never** ask Google to remove a review because it is negative — that only works
for policy violations, and a visible mix is what makes the profile credible.

---

## Track it

Keep a simple list: name, service, area, date asked, date followed up, whether
it landed. Two things to watch — that the **areas are spread** (all thirty from
DHA teaches Google nothing about Johar Town), and that the **services are mixed**
(nurse, attendant, drip visit, post-op), so the profile reads as a full service
rather than a one-trick one.
